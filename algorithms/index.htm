About This Page

This page is a work in progress, both in terms of content and presentation.

UC Irvine is on a quarter system; this is a ten-week class. The class size is often several hundred students.

One prerequisite is a data structures class, an early sophomore level course covering core data structures (such as binary search trees, hash tables, and binary heaps), graph fundamentals, asymptotic notation, and sorting algorithms. Other prerequisites are Discrete Math; this includes Boolean algebra, proofs (induction and otherwise), combinatorics, and recurrence relations. This also briefly covers some topics that will be revisited in depth in Theory of Computation. Students must also have completed two quarters of Calculus.

In Spring quarter of 2025, I made a significant change to how I teach the class to more fully incorporate active learning into the class. This was something I had wanted to do for a few years. I am continuing to refine and improve my approach.

In addition to (typically PhD student) TAs, my offerings of algorithms make meaningful use of UCI's excellent Learning Assistants program. These are excellent undergraduates who get pedagogical training and practice these techniques by assisting with active learning in classes with high enrollment.

Other Notes

The copyright notice on some or all PDFs is an artifact from an era where I needed such a statement in order to send takedown notices when students would upload my handouts (or homework assignments, etc) to websites where I would prefer my materials not be uploaded. Anyone teaching an algorithms course is more than welcome to take anything from my materials, but please be aware of a few things.
  • I did not create all of the material myself, nor do I claim to do so. Offhand, I believe I have used at least six different algorithms textbooks thus far in my career. I have also taken material from other algorithms instructors.
  • I am not posting solutions for these on this webpage.
